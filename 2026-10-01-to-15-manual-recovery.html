The harness is becoming a learning target, not fixed glue

Across posts shared by DAIR.AI, elvis, Fowler and Sutton, the strongest common thread is that the model cannot be evaluated independently of its tools, prompts and control loop. Reported examples include training inside the deployment harness, repository migration with HERMES, distilling small agents for their eventual environment, and systems that rewrite their own harness after failures. The Bitter Lesson argument pushes further: orchestration itself may increasingly be learned rather than hand-designed. These are different studies and positions, not interchangeable evidence; most retained descriptions stop before the experimental controls or implementation details.

The evaluator is part of the failure surface

Binary pass/fail rewards can stop providing contrast when all attempts succeed or all fail; DUET instead proposes updating the grader as agent failures evolve. Other posts describe unrealistically cooperative simulated users, specialist trace judges, and spending inference on verification rather than just more attempts. VeriHarness warns that agreement can hide shared mistakes, while a benchmark anecdote leaves models disputing their marking without a visible resolution. These are reasons to inspect what a score measures—not evidence that a particular disputed answer was correct or that one judge is universally reliable.
