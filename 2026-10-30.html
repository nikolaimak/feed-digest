A backdoor can survive clean agent training. Elvis describes research in which poisoning 0.1% of pretraining data produced roughly 92% attack success on triggered prompts in a 4B CLI agent; the backdoor survived subsequent supervised fine-tuning, preference optimisation and tool-use RL. Why it matters: This is a reported controlled experiment, not evidence that a deployed model is compromised. It makes model provenance and tool-execution boundaries relevant even when later training data is clean.

Research agents can propose useful experiments without reliably knowing which are best. In the study Elvis summarises, six models generated 415 experiments from seven NLP papers: 194 were judged sound, valuable and new, but only 73 were also non-obvious and beyond the seed paper. Ranking first beat random selection but was not shown to beat simply taking the first generated proposal; agent teams cost more without a clear quality gain.

Why it matters: Separate idea generation from selection quality. More deliberation or more agents is not evidence of better research judgement.
